# 我是人

我喜欢玩木雕。

有一天我买了一块木头，我满怀欣喜地给了它一锯子，它竟然...还是一块木头！我很好奇，又给它锯了八下，它竟然...变成了一块砂狼白子形状的木头！就是不知道为什么有些部位无论从哪个角度看都有马赛克...这TM是虚拟世界吗，为什么我自己雕的也会被和谐啊？

僵王博士让我眉毛以下全截肢，他说只要砍得够狠，锯到只剩个脑壳，绝对没有马赛克。我试了试还真是，怪不得他有个很大的脑子，但是我实在认不出来这个天灵盖是白子酱。

幸好我提前CtrlCV备份了一下，我猜是因为我的锯子不够好，于是找二舅妈借了各种各样的膨胀螺丝，把锯子爆改成了凿子。白子酱被我雕得很漂亮，但是我不管凿多少下那里还是打着厚码。

我终于力竭了，瘫坐在地上，这时我的鞋带跳起来给了我一巴掌，正常人哪能受得了这么巨大的心理伤害，我直接给它打了四个死结，但它还是不肯就范，我只好把它缠在了雕像的脖子上，那一刻就好像缺的韭菜盒子终于补上了一样，戴着围巾的白子酱在灯光下闪闪发光。鞋带，哦不围巾求我不要杀他，作为报答他会赠送我泳装皮肤和限定皮肤。

我高兴极了，但是换皮并没有消除马赛克，这是我的第一次失望。我望着手里的凿子，疯狂地向他诉说着内心的委屈，凿子没有安慰我，也没有数落我，他只是和白子酱一起，静静地听着我的牢骚。世事无常，谁又能主宰马赛克的生命呢？

日子一天天过去了，我和凿子酱的关系越来越好了，白子是谁啊真不熟，我似乎早就忘记了凿子被制造出来的本来目的。为了给大家分享我和凿子酱的幸福生活，我让全世界的人免费到我家参观凿子酱，有人很喜欢凿子酱，有人痛恨凿子酱，还有人指着凿子酱的身体和其他人大声争吵。

我听不懂他们在吵什么，但我不想给大家带去不愉快的体验，就想着去劝架。但是当我的视线重新看向那个凿子时，我崩溃了，那哪里是一个凿子啊，那分明是一个锯子上面长满了数不清的膨胀螺丝，无论怎么看从哪里看都无限接近于一个凿子，但是锯子和膨胀螺丝不可能是一把凿子。我大受震撼，连忙跑到木雕白子酱旁边，乞求她的原谅。

在梦里，我不躲，不藏，不绕，上帝稳稳地接住了我。上帝告诉我祂眼里是没有马赛克的，我不知道祂说的是不是真的，但我此时完全理解僵王博士了，他和上帝都有一颗鬼脑。

我醒了，手里依然攥着那个被称为“凿子”的东西，我想起自己的使命了。

# *I Am Human*

I enjoy woodcarving.

One day I bought a block of wood. Full of excitement, I gave it one stroke of the saw, and it turned out to be... still a block of wood! Curious, I gave it eight more strokes, and it turned out to be... a block of wood shaped like Sunaokami Shiroko! Except for some reason, certain parts had mosaic censoring no matter what angle you viewed them from. Is this some kind of The Matrix? Why is something I carved myself getting censored?

Dr. Zomboss told me to amputate everything below the eyebrows. He said as long as you cut hard enough — saw it down to just the skull — there would be absolutely no mosaic censoring. I tried it, and sure enough it worked. No wonder he has such a large brain. But I honestly couldn't recognize that skullcap as Shiroko-chan.

Fortunately I had made a Ctrl+C/Ctrl+V backup in advance. I figured my saw just wasn't good enough, so I borrowed all kinds of expansion bolts from my second aunt-in-law and modded the saw into a chisel. I carved Shiroko-chan beautifully, but no matter how many times I chiseled, that area was still covered in mosaic censoring.

I finally ran out of strength and collapsed onto the floor. At that moment, my shoelace leapt up and slapped me across the face. No normal person could withstand such enormous emotional damage. I immediately tied it into four dead knots, but it still refused to submit, so I wound it around the statue's neck. In that moment it felt just like a missing piece of chive pastry had finally been filled in — Shiroko-chan wearing a scarf, gleaming under the lamplight. The shoelace — or rather, the scarf — begged me not to kill it, and as a reward, it offered me a swimsuit skin and a festival skin.

I was overjoyed, but changing skins did nothing to remove the mosaic censoring. This was my first disappointment. I stared at the chisel in my hands and poured out all my inner grievances to it in a frenzy. The chisel did not comfort me, nor did it scold me. It simply listened, together with Shiroko-chan, quietly, as I rambled on. The world is unpredictable — who can ever rule over the life of mosaic censoring?

Days went by, and my relationship with Chisel-chan grew closer and closer. Who was Shiroko again? Don't really know her. It seemed I had long since forgotten the original purpose for which the chisel was made. To share my happy life with Chisel-chan with the world, I invited everyone to visit Chisel-chan at my home, free of charge. Some people loved Chisel-chan; some despised Chisel-chan; others stood before Chisel-chan's body and argued loudly with each other.

I couldn't understand what they were arguing about, but not wanting to bring anyone an unpleasant experience, I thought I'd go and mediate. But when my gaze returned to that chisel, I broke down. That wasn't a chisel at all — it was plainly a saw covered in countless expansion bolts, which, no matter how you looked at it or from whatever angle, infinitely approximated a chisel — yet a saw covered in expansion bolts cannot be a chisel. Deeply shaken, I rushed to the wooden carving of Shiroko-chan and begged her forgiveness.

In my dream, I did not hide, I did not flee, I did not dodge. God steadily caught me. God told me there was no mosaic censoring in His eyes. I didn't know whether what He said was true, but in that moment I understood Dr. Zomboss completely — both he and God possessed a ghostly kind of brain.

I woke up, still clutching the thing called a "chisel" in my hand. I remembered my purpose.
